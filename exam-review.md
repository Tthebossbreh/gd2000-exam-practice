## 1. Is it a game?

The course defines a game as "a voluntary, structured play activity that
players do for fun." Pick one of Cookie Clicker, The Stanley Parable, or
Minecraft in Creative Mode. Argue whether it is a game under that definition,
and say which part of the definition it stresses or breaks.

Minecraft creative mode is decidedly a game, albeit with heavy stressing on "structured." While some may argue it does not have a gameplay loop in the common sense, players are still able to explore, construct, and do their own thing, rewarded with satisfaction rather than a tangible reward.

## 2. Three games

Pick three games we have not covered in class. For each, give its core skill
(dexterity, logic/calculation, social/deception, spatial, chance), its player
alignment (1v1, team vs team, co-op, 1-vs-many, solo), and its information
state (perfect or imperfect). Then say which of the three you would rather
design, and why.

ARK: Survival Ascended's primary skill is dexterity, with a one versus many player alignment and an imperfect information state. Pokemon is primarily logic and calculation, 1v1 player alignment, with imperfect information. Magic: The Gathering's primary skill is chance, with 1v1-3 player alignment and an imperfect information state. I would rather design a game like pokemon, because the idea of a one-on-one confrontation based solely on wit makes for the most satisfying experience.

## 3. Chess and Texas Hold'em

Both are two-player competitive games. Describe how hidden information and
randomness change what the player is actually doing in each. Then give one
concrete design consequence: something you would have to change if you moved
chess's rules to poker's information model, or the other way around.

Hidden information and randomness have poker players bluffing, calculating potential hands, and playing around chances. To make chess have hidden information, you could make it so that the opponent could only move one type of piece, decided the move prior, and only they knew about it.

## 5. The game loop

Pick one of: Go, Blackjack, Settlers of Catan, Pandemic, Slay the Spire,
Hades, Minecraft, Stardew Valley, Overcooked, Pac-Man.

Describe its game loop: what the player does, what the game updates in
response, and what feedback the player gets. Then remove one step of the loop
and describe what breaks.

Game: Minecraft

Minecraft's gameplay loop is discovering materials that can increase your ability to discover materials, such as wood allowing you to mine stone, allowing you to mine iron, and so on. If you remove the step of increasing the number of discoverable materials, then the player is stuck at a wooden level, and thus has no reason to progress.

## 6. Autobattler design

Dota Underlords and Hearthstone Battlegrounds are auto-battlers. Pick one
design rule -- for example, that a triple can only be ranked up once, or that
damage scales with the number of surviving units -- and explain the trade-off
it makes: what it encourages in the player, and what it costs.

The idea of damage being based on the number of surviving units is a tradeoff, because while it does reward good planning and teambuilding, it can allow for snowballing quite easily, with the player on top steamrolling those below them.
